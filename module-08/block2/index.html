📖READ: THE PORCH LIGHT IN NOVEMBER
A story about family, waiting and home.⌄

LEAD-IN

  1. Is there a light in your home that someone left on for you?
  2. What does “home” mean to you — a place, a person, or a feeling?
  3. The phrasal verb to leave on means to keep something (like a light) burning. Why do people leave lights on for those they love?

THE TEXT

It was November. Cold. Grey. The kind of month that makes you want to stay inside.

My brother, Jake, left three years ago. Not because he was angry. Not because we fought. He just… needed to go. He said he’d come back in a year or two.

That was three years ago.

I still remember the morning he left. His boots by the back door, mud still dried on them from the Fourth of July when we’d walked through the fields together. His jacket, thrown over the chair, as if he’d just stepped out for a minute.

“I’ll follow in a year or two,” I said.

He laughed and circled a date in blue on the calendar. We both pretended that date meant something.

Every night since, I’ve left the porch light on. It’s a small thing, a single bulb in a glass case. But when November comes, and the days get short, that light feels like the only warm thing in the world.

My father calls sometimes. He asks if I’m eating. If I’m sleeping. I change the subject to the trees, to the weather, to anything but the empty chair at the table.

Because the truth is, I keep setting one more place. Every night. A plate, a fork, a glass. For a brother who isn’t here.

I’ve driven past his exit a hundred times. Just to prove to myself that I still know the way. I sing along with the engine, the words I was too proud to say: “Come home.”

The moths find the porch light. They circle it, confused, drawn to something warm. Every time I see one, I feel a little like that moth — circling a memory, waiting for something that might not come back.

But I keep the light on.

Because if he ever crosses that county line, if he ever sees that little glow from the road, he’ll know. The porch light in November means there’s still a way back home.

♥FOR THE HEART AND MIND
Comprehension, analysis and personal connection.⌄

A. COMPREHENSION CHECK

  1. When did the narrator's brother leave?
  2. What did the brother say about coming back?
  3. What does the narrator do every night?
  4. What does the narrator's father ask about on the phone?
  5. What does the narrator keep doing at the dinner table?
  6. Why does the narrator drive past his brother's exit?
  7. What do the moths do around the porch light?
  8. What does the porch light in November mean?

B. THINK & ANALYSE

  1. The narrator says: “We both pretended that date meant something.” Why do people pretend when they know the truth?
  2. Why does the narrator change the subject when his father asks if he's eating and sleeping?
  3. What does the moth represent in this story? Is the narrator like the moth?
  4. The final line says: “The porch light in November means there's still a way back home.” Is “home” a place or a feeling?

C. DISCUSS & RELATE

  1. Is there someone you are waiting for? How do you show them you are waiting?
  2. Why is it hard to say “come home” to someone who left?
  3. What small things do families do to show love without words?
✎FOR LANGUAGE SKILLS
Phrasal verbs, expressions, past tenses and creative practice.⌄

A. VOCABULARY FOCUS — PHRASAL VERBS & EXPRESSIONS

  1. Find the phrasal verbs in the text:
    1. to leave on — ___________
    2. to drive past — ___________
    3. to change the subject — ___________
  2. Complete the sentences with the correct word:
    1. Every night, I ______ the porch light ______ for my brother.
    2. I keep ______ one more place at the table.
    3. The moths are ______ to the warm light.
    4. I ______ past his exit just to feel close to him.
  3. Make your own sentences with these expressions: to leave on, to set a place, to be drawn to.

B. GRAMMAR IN CONTEXT — PAST TENSES & USED TO

  1. Find examples of Past Simple and Past Continuous in the text:
    • Past Simple (completed actions): ______, ______, ______
    • Past Continuous (background actions): ______, ______
  2. Used to for past habits: “We used to walk through the fields together.” Complete: My brother ______ (leave) his boots by the back door.

C. BRAIN WORKOUT

  1. The empty chair: Draw or describe the dinner table with the empty chair. What does it look like? What feelings does it create?
  2. Letter to a brother: Write a short letter (5–7 sentences) from the narrator to Jake, saying the words he was too proud to say.
  3. Memory chain: Students take turns adding one detail to the story of the day Jake left.

🎵 SONG EXERCISES

EXERCISE 1. The Language of Waiting.

Match the symbol with its meaning: 1. The porch light — a) memories that are still present, like ghosts; 2. The boots by the door — b) the small gesture of hope that someone will return; 3. The coffee ring ghosts — c) the act of leaving — but also the possibility of return; 4. The empty chair — e) the person who is missing from the family; 5. The moth circling the light — d) someone who keeps circling around what they've lost. Then write 2–3 sentences: Which symbol touches you the most? Why?

EXERCISE 2. The Power of Small Gestures. Think about someone you miss. What is a “porch light” in your life? Do you have an “empty chair” in your life? What do you do to remember them? Write 4–5 sentences about one small gesture that means a lot to you.

EXERCISE 3. The Road Home. What does “home” mean in this song — a place, a person, or a feeling? Why is November significant? Have you ever felt “away from home” emotionally? Complete: “For me, the porch light in November is…”
