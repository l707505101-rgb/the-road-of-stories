📖READ: THE ANONYMOUS HELPER
Read the full story about Mark, a man who helps others anonymously.⌄

BEFORE YOU READ

  1. Have you ever received help from a stranger? How did it make you feel?
  2. If you could do something kind for someone anonymously, what would you do?
  3. The phrasal verb to give away can mean to reveal a secret. Why might someone want to keep their kindness a secret?

THE TEXT

It started with small things.

An elderly woman in a small town found a bag of groceries on her doorstep. No note. No name. Just food.

A single mother received an envelope with cash and a short message: “For your son's school trip. He deserves it.”

A struggling student opened his email to find that someone had paid for his online course. The receipt showed a gift card, no name.

People in the town began to talk. Who was helping them? A rich relative? A charity? A ghost?

The local newspaper picked up the story. They called the mysterious helper “The Anonymous Angel.” Articles were written. Interviews were given by grateful recipients. But no one knew who it was.

Then one day, the local computer repair shop owner came forward. He had noticed something strange. Someone had been using a public computer at the library late at night. The searches were all about people in need — single parents, sick children, elderly people living alone.

“Whoever it was,” the shop owner said, “they didn't want to be found.”

A young reporter named Lena decided to investigate. She spent weeks following small clues. Finally, she tracked the helper to a small apartment on the edge of town.

She knocked on the door. A middle-aged man opened it. His clothes were simple. His apartment was small. He looked... ordinary.

“Are you the one?” Lena asked.

The man sighed. “Please, come in. But I won't give you a story.”

His name was Mark. He worked as a warehouse worker. He wasn't rich. He wasn't famous. He just believed in doing good.

“I had a difficult childhood,” he explained. “A stranger once helped my family when we had nothing. I never knew who it was. But it changed my life. Now I have a little extra money. Not much. But enough to help someone else.”

He didn't use his own computer. He used the public library computer so no one could track him. He paid with gift cards. He never left his name.

“But why anonymous?” Lena asked. “People want to thank you.”

Mark shook his head. “If people know who I am, they'll want to thank me. They'll want to repay me. That's not why I do it. I do it because... because I can. Because someone did it for me. The joy is in the helping, not in the recognition.”

Lena didn't write the article. She couldn't betray his trust. But she started volunteering at a local shelter on weekends.

“Some secrets,” she later told a friend, “are worth keeping.”

And the anonymous help continued. Groceries appeared. Bills were paid. Scholarships were funded. The town still talked about their “Angel.” But no one ever found out who it was.

Except Lena. And she never told.

♥FOR THE HEART AND MIND
Comprehension, thinking deeper and discussing the ideas.⌄

A. COMPREHENSION CHECK

  1. What kind of things did the anonymous helper do for people in the town?
  2. What did the local newspaper call this mysterious person?
  3. How did the computer repair shop owner help the investigation?
  4. Who was the anonymous helper? What was his job?
  5. Why did Mark start helping others?
  6. Why didn't Mark want people to know who he was?
  7. What did Lena decide to do after meeting Mark?
  8. What does the final line mean: “Some secrets are worth keeping”?

B. THINK & ANALYSE

  1. Mark says: “The joy is in the helping, not in the recognition.” Do you agree? Why or why not?
  2. Why did Lena choose not to write the article? Was she right or wrong?
  3. The text says Mark “used the public library computer so no one could track him.” How do technologies help people hide their identity? Is this always bad?
  4. Mark was inspired by a stranger who helped his family when he was a child. Can one act of kindness create a chain reaction?

C. DISCUSS & RELATE

  1. If you had a little extra money, would you prefer to help anonymously or publicly? Why?
  2. Do you think social media makes people want recognition for their good deeds? Is that a problem?
  3. Lena started volunteering at a shelter instead of writing the story. How did Mark's example change her?

B. GRAMMAR IN CONTEXT — PASSIVE VOICE & PAST TENSES

  1. Find examples of the Past Simple Passive in the text. Example: A bag of groceries was found on her doorstep.
  2. Rewrite in the Passive Voice:
    1. Someone left an envelope with cash. → An envelope with cash ___________ left.
    2. Someone paid for his online course. → His online course ___________ paid for.
    3. A stranger once helped his family. → His family ___________ helped by a stranger.
  3. Complete with Past Simple or Past Perfect:
    1. Mark explained that he ___________ (have) a difficult childhood.
    2. Before Lena ___________ (track) Mark down, many articles ___________ (write) about the “Angel”.
    3. She ___________ (knock) on his door after she ___________ (find) his address.

C. BRAIN WORKOUT — PHRASAL VERBS WITH GIVE & TURN

  1. Complete with the correct particle:
    1. Mark didn't want to give ______ his identity. (reveal)
    2. He turned ______ to helping others after a stranger helped him. (changed direction/started doing)
    3. Many people turned ______ to the library to try to find clues. (appeared/arrived)
    4. The town gave ______ to the “Angel” as a kind of legend. (created a name)
  2. Create your own sentences using these phrasal verbs in the context of kindness: give away, turn to, give back.
  3. Role Play: In pairs, act out the conversation between Lena and Mark when she first knocks on his door. One is the persistent reporter, the other is the humble helper.
☆BEYOND THE TEXT
Creative tasks and real-world connection.⌄

Choose ONE:

  1. Lena's Diary: Write a short diary entry from Lena the night she met Mark. Why did she decide not to write the article?
  2. Mark's Letter (Unsent): Mark was inspired by a stranger who helped his family. Write a short letter from Mark to that unknown stranger, thanking them.
  3. Social Media Post (Irony): Write a short, ironic social media post that Mark might secretly laugh at — a post where someone is getting praise for being “so generous” publicly.
  4. AI Image Prompt: Create a prompt for an image of Mark in his small apartment, looking at a gift card on his simple wooden table, with a soft lamp light. He looks peaceful, not rich. The mood is quiet, humble, wise.
    Example: “A middle-aged man in a simple, clean but small apartment sitting at a wooden table. He holds a blank gift card in his hands. A single warm lamp lights his face. His expression is peaceful, content, not sad. Outside the window, a night city is blurred. Style: photorealistic, cinematic lighting, warm tones.”
  5. Anonymous Kindness Challenge: For one week, try to do one small anonymous act of kindness (leave a note, help without being asked, give something without taking credit). Write 3–4 sentences about how it made you feel.

♧ THE KINDNESS CHAIN GROUP PROJECT

Class project: “The Kindness Chain”

Type: Kindness action + reflection   Duration: 1 week

Integration: English + social responsibility + emotional intelligence

Your Mission
Perform one small act of kindness for someone — anonymously. Then, write a short reflection about how it felt.
Step by step ▾
  1. Choose your act of kindness.
  2. Do it anonymously — don't tell the person it was you.
  3. Write a short reflection (5–7 sentences) in English answering the questions below.
  4. Share your reflection with the class (you can keep the act anonymous).
Ideas for anonymous kindness:
  • Leave a kind note on someone's desk.
  • Pay for someone's coffee or snack secretly.
  • Help a classmate with homework without taking credit.
  • Leave a small treat on a teacher's desk.
  • Do a chore at home without being asked.
  • Send an anonymous encouraging message to someone who seems sad.
  • Donate something you don't need to a charity box.
Reflection questions:
  1. What did you do? (Don't reveal the person's name if you want to stay anonymous.)
  2. How did you feel while doing it?
  3. How did you feel after doing it?
  4. Was it difficult to stay anonymous? Why or why not?
  5. Would you do it again? Why?
