THE TEXT: “WHAT IS WEALTH?” COLLAPSE⌃

OPEN / CLOSE COMPLETE TEXT

VOICE 1. Ancient Rome (Seneca, 4 BC–65 AD, philosopher)
“It is not the man who has too little, but the man who craves more, that is poor. Poverty is not having too little; it is wanting more. Wealth consists not in having great possessions, but in having few wants. A man is rich in proportion to the number of things he can afford to let alone.”

VOICE 2. 19th Century America (Henry David Thoreau, 1817–1862, writer and naturalist)
“I went to the woods because I wished to live deliberately, to front only the essential facts of life, and see if I could not learn what it had to teach, and not, when I came to die, discover that I had not lived. Most of the luxuries, and many of the so-called comforts of life, are not only not indispensable, but positive hindrances to the elevation of mankind. A man is rich in proportion to the number of things which he can afford to let alone.” — from Walden; or, Life in the Woods

VOICE 3. Modern Sweden (The Law of Jante / Lagom)
In Scandinavian culture, there is an unspoken rule: “Lagom är bäst” — “The right amount is best.” Not too much, not too little. Enough. There is also the Law of Jante, which warns against the belief that you are better than others. Its deeper meaning here is about equality of worth. No one is above, no one is beneath. Wealth is not showing off; wealth is having exactly what you need — and no more than your neighbour.

VOICE 4. Africa (Zulu Proverb, South Africa)
“Umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu.”
This means: “A person is a person through other people.” A Zulu elder explained it this way: “I am because we are. Your wealth is not in your bank account. Your wealth is in your relationships. If you have many people who love you, you are a billionaire. If you are alone with your money, you are a beggar.”

VOICE 5. A Modern Voice (You)
After reading these four voices, complete the sentence:
“To me, real wealth means ______________________________.”
