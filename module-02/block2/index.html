OPEN THE LETTER

January 15, 2025

Dear Future Me,

I'm writing this at 3 a.m. I can't sleep. Today I bought something I'd saved for — new wireless headphones. $180. They sound amazing. Everyone at school wants them.

But here's the thing. When I took them out of the box, I felt… nothing. Just a quick “cool” and then I opened Instagram.

I remembered how, when I was a kid, a new pencil case could make me happy for weeks. Now I buy things, feel nothing, and immediately want more.

So I have questions for you, Future Me.

Do you still buy things to impress people you don't even like?
Do you still scroll through ads at 2 a.m. and feel like your life is incomplete without some shiny gadget?
Do you know the difference between wanting something and needing it?

I hope you've learned.

I hope you spend money on time with people you love, not just things.
I hope you pay for experiences — concerts, trips, a cooking class with someone you care about.
I hope you keep a “treasure box” like Grandma's cookie memories, not a shelf of gadgets you forgot about.

And I hope you're not in debt. Please, Future Me. Debt is a cage.

Right now, I'm 16. I don't have much money. But I have something else: I still have time to become someone who spends wisely, kindly, freely.

I want to be rich. But not in the way the ads promise.

Yours,
Me (16 years old)

P.S. If you're 26 and you still haven't called Grandma enough — call her. Tomorrow. She doesn't need headphones. She needs you.
