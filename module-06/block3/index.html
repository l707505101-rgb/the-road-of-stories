THE LAST
SUPPER CLUB

A philosophical and humorous collage of reviews & interviews
STORY FOCUS
Food is not just ingredients. It is atmosphere, people, and stories. Sometimes the strangest dinner is the one you remember for life.
LEVEL: ADVANCED READ · LISTEN · THINK · CREATE REVIEWS & INTERVIEW PHRASAL VERBS WITH GIVE CONDITIONALS
📖 THE TEXT
LEAD-IN
  1. What's the strangest restaurant you've ever been to?
  2. If a restaurant had no menu, would you go? Why?
  3. The phrasal verb to give over can mean to dedicate entirely to something. What would you give over your life to?

The Last Supper Club is a small restaurant in a quiet street. It has no sign, no menu, and no prices. You can only get in if someone who has been there invites you. We asked three people about their experience.

REVIEW 1 — Sarah, 24, graphic designer

Listen to Sarah's review.

“If I told you what I ate, you wouldn't believe me. There was no menu. The chef just came out and asked: ‘What do you need?’ Not ‘What do you want?’ — ‘What do you need?’

I said I didn't know. He smiled and said: ‘If you don't know what you need, you've come to the right place.’

He brought me soup. It tasted like my grandmother's kitchen. I cried. If I hadn't cried, I wouldn't have believed it was real.

I've been back three times. If they gave me a membership card, I'd frame it.

REVIEW 2 — James, 47, taxi driver

Listen to James's review.

“Look, I'm not a fancy guy. If you give me a burger, I'm happy. My daughter took me there for my birthday. I almost gave up and left when I saw no menu.

But then this old woman came out — the chef's mother, I think. She looked at me and said: ‘You work hard. You're tired. You need something that reminds you why you work.’

She brought me bread and butter. Just bread and butter. But it was the best bread I've ever had. If I could give you the taste, I would. But you have to go yourself.

I gave in and tried everything. And you know what? I felt like a king. If you ever get invited, don't give up before you try.

REVIEW 3 — Maria, 19, student

Listen to Maria's review.

“I'm vegetarian. I told them when I booked. They said: ‘We don't do special diets. We do what you need.’

I was nervous. If they gave me meat, I thought, I'd have to leave.

The first dish was mushrooms. Just mushrooms, cooked in butter with garlic. If I closed my eyes, I was in a forest. The second was pasta with tomatoes that tasted like sunshine. The third was chocolate — dark, rich, perfect.

After dinner, the chef came out. I asked: ‘How did you know?’

He said: ‘If you listen to people, they tell you everything without words. You needed to feel safe. So we gave you comfort.’

I've never felt so seen in my life. If I had more money, I'd eat there every week. But maybe that would ruin it. Some things are special because you can't have them often.

INTERVIEW WITH THE CHEF

We asked the chef, a quiet man in his sixties, about his philosophy.

Chef: “People think cooking is about recipes. It's not. It's about giving people what they didn't know they needed.

If you give someone exactly what they ask for, they'll be satisfied. But if you give them what they need, they'll be changed.”

Q: How do you know what they need?

Chef: “I watch. I listen. If a man comes in alone on a Tuesday, he doesn't need a steak. He needs to feel less alone. So I give him bread that reminds him of his mother.

If a couple is silent, they don't need fancy wine. They need something to laugh about. So I give them a dish that surprises them.”

Q: What about the ones who don't understand?

Chef: “Some people give up before they begin. They see no menu and they leave. That's okay. They weren't ready.

But the ones who stay? They give themselves over to the experience. And that's when the magic happens.”

THE FRIENDS' VERDICT

We told Mike, Dave, and the Narrator about this place.

Mike: “If I ever get invited, I'm going. I'll eat whatever they give me.”

Dave: “If they give you something weird, don't blame me.”

Narrator: “If we all go together, it'll be an adventure. And if it's terrible, at least we'll have a story.”

Mike: “With us, there's always a story.”

❤️ FOR THE HEART AND MIND

A. Comprehension Check

  1. How do you get into The Last Supper Club?
  2. What did Sarah cry about? Why?
  3. What did James almost do when he saw there was no menu?
  4. What did the old woman bring James? How did he feel?
  5. What did Maria worry about before her meal?
  6. What three dishes did Maria get?
  7. According to the chef, what's the difference between giving people what they ask for and giving them what they need?
  8. What does the chef do when a man comes in alone on a Tuesday?

B. Think & Analyse

  1. Sarah says: “If I hadn't cried, I wouldn't have believed it was real.” What does this say about the power of food to connect us to memories?
  2. James says: “If I could give you the taste, I would. But you have to go yourself.” Why can't you fully describe some experiences?
  3. Maria says: “Some things are special because you can't have them often.” Do you agree? Can too much of a good thing ruin it?
  4. The chef says: “If you listen to people, they tell you everything without words.” What does this mean? Have you ever felt “heard” without words?
  5. The friends say: “If it's terrible, at least we'll have a story.” Is a good story worth a bad meal?

C. Discuss & Relate

  1. If you could go to a restaurant where the chef decided what you need, what do you think they would serve you?
  2. Is there a food that reminds you of a specific person or memory?
  3. Do you agree that the best meals are about more than just food? What else matters?

C. Brain Workout

  1. Write your own review: Imagine you went to The Last Supper Club. Write a short review (4–5 sentences) using at least two conditionals.
  2. The chef's question: The chef asks: “What do you need?” Write your answer in 2–3 sentences.
  3. Role play: In pairs, one is the chef, one is a guest. The chef asks “What do you need?” The guest answers. The chef then decides what to serve and explains why.
