GRANDMA'S
SECRET INGREDIENT

A warm family story with humour
STORY FOCUS
Sometimes the perfect recipe isn't about exact proportions — it's about memories and love.
LEVEL: SIMPLEREAD · LISTEN · THINK · CREATEPHRASAL VERBS WITH GIVECONDITIONALS
📖 THE STORY
Before you read:
  1. Do you have a favourite family recipe? Who makes it best?
  2. What happens when you try to cook something without the recipe?
  3. The phrasal verb to give up means to stop trying. When was the last time you almost gave up in the kitchen?

After our travels, we decided to have a quiet weekend at home. Mike had an idea.

“My grandmother's apple pie,” he said. “It's the best in the world. If you try it, you'll understand what happiness tastes like.”

Dave raised an eyebrow. “Can you make it?”

“Of course! She gave me the recipe. If I follow it exactly, it will be perfect.”

Famous last words.

We went to the supermarket. Mike gave the list to Dave, who checked everything twice. Flour, sugar, butter, apples, cinnamon. “If we forget something,” Dave said, “we'll have to go back.” So we didn't forget.

Back at Mike's apartment, we started. The kitchen was small, but we didn't give up. Mike read the recipe aloud:

“Mix flour and butter until it looks like breadcrumbs.”

We mixed. It looked like breadcrumbs. Good.

“Add sugar and a pinch of salt.”

We added. Still good.

“Add cold water and form a dough.”

Mike added the water. Too much water. The dough became soup. “If I add more flour,” he said, “it will be fine.” He added flour. Too much flour. Now it was a dry lump.

Dave sighed. “If you had measured the water, this wouldn't have happened.”

Mike didn't give in. “It's fine! We can fix it!”

He added more butter. Then more flour. Then more water. The dough looked confused. We were confused.

Narrator: “Maybe we should give up and buy a pie?”

Mike: “Real men don't give up!”

Finally, after an hour of wrestling, we had something shaped like a pie. It went into the oven.

Thirty minutes later, we took it out. It looked… interesting. Some parts were burned. Some parts were still dough. But it smelled like apples and cinnamon.

We cut a slice. Mike took the first bite. His face went through many emotions: hope, doubt, surprise, then a smile.

“It tastes like…” he paused. “It tastes like my grandmother's kitchen. Not exactly her pie. But the feeling.”

Dave tried it. “If you hadn't added that extra water, it might have been terrible. But this chaos… it's actually good.”

We ate the whole thing. It was imperfect, lopsided, burned in places. But it was ours. And it was delicious.

Later, Mike called his grandmother. “Grandma, what's your secret ingredient?”

She laughed. “Love, dear. And a little bit of chaos. If you don't make mistakes, you don't make memories.”

Mike grinned. “Then we made a lot of memories.”

❤️ FOR THE HEART AND MIND

A. Comprehension Check

  1. What did Mike want to make?
  2. Who gave him the recipe?
  3. What went wrong with the dough?
  4. How did Mike try to fix it?
  5. What did the finished pie look like?
  6. How did it taste?
  7. What did Mike's grandmother say was the secret ingredient?

B. Think & Analyse

  1. Mike said: “If I follow it exactly, it will be perfect.” Why do you think it wasn’t perfect?
  2. Dave said: “If you had measured the water, this wouldn’t have happened.” What type of conditional is this? What does it express?
  3. The grandmother said: “If you don’t make mistakes, you don’t make memories.” Do you agree? Why?

C. Discuss & Relate

  1. Have you ever tried to cook something and it went wrong? What happened?
  2. Is there a food that reminds you of your childhood or family?
  3. Why do you think “imperfect” food made with love can taste better than perfect food from a shop?
🧠 FOR LANGUAGE SKILLS

A. Vocabulary Focus — Phrasal verbs with GIVE

1. Match.
1. give up — a) stop resisting, yield
2. give in — b) stop trying
3. give out — c) distribute or stop functioning
4. give away — d) reveal a secret
5. give back — e) return something
2. Fill in the blanks with the correct form of GIVE + particle.
  1. After three hours of cooking, they almost ______.
  2. Mike didn't ______ to Dave's doubts; he kept going.
  3. If you ______ the recipe, it won't be a secret anymore.
  4. The oven ______ halfway through, but the pie was already done.
  5. Can you ______ my baking tray? I lent it to you last week.
3. Make your own sentences with three different phrasal verbs from the list.

B. Grammar in Context — Conditionals

1. Zero Conditional: Find “If you don't make mistakes, you don't make memories.”
Complete: If you ______ (mix) flour and water, you ______ (get) dough.
2. First Conditional: Find “If I follow it exactly, it will be perfect.”
Complete: If we ______ (forget) something, we ______ (have) to go back.
3. Third Conditional: Find “If you had measured the water, this wouldn't have happened.”
Complete: If Mike ______ (not / add) too much water, the dough ______ (be) perfect.
4. Identify the conditional types.
  1. “If you try it, you’ll understand what happiness tastes like.” — Type _____
  2. “If I add more flour, it will be fine.” — Type _____
  3. “If you had measured the water, this wouldn’t have happened.” — Type _____

C. Brain Workout

  1. Chain story: In groups, continue: “If Mike’s grandmother had been there, she would have…”
  2. Recipe translation: Take a simple recipe from your culture and explain it in English using conditionals. (“If you want it spicy, add…”)
  3. Role play: In pairs, act out Mike calling his grandmother to tell her about the pie disaster.
🌱 BEYOND THE TEXT

Choose ONE:

  1. Write a recipe: Write your own recipe for something simple (sandwich, salad, etc.). Use conditionals.
  2. Grandma's letter: Write a short letter from Mike's grandmother to him after the phone call.

  4. Your own kitchen disaster: Write a short paragraph about a time you (or someone you know) had a kitchen disaster.
